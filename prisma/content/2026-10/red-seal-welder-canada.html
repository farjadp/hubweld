A Red Seal welder is a certified journeyperson who has passed the interprovincial Red Seal exam for the welder trade: 125 multiple-choice questions with a 70% pass mark. You get there through a provincial apprenticeship. In Ontario (trade 456A) that is about 6,000 hours over three levels; in British Columbia it is 4,620 work hours plus three levels of school.

Key takeaways

  • Welder is a Red Seal trade. The provinces issue the certificate; the Red Seal endorsement is what lets other provinces recognise it.
  • The Red Seal welder exam has 125 questions in four blocks. Welding processes alone make up 54 of them.
  • Ontario's 456A apprenticeship is about 5,280 on-the-job hours plus 720 in-school hours.
  • Alberta's apprenticeship runs 3 years, each with at least 1,560 hours on the job and 8 weeks of technical training.
  • A Red Seal and a CWB ticket are different credentials. Many employers ask for both.

The Red Seal is the closest thing Canada has to a national welder licence. It is also widely misunderstood, often confused with a CWB ticket, and handled slightly differently in every province. This guide explains how the Red Seal welder pathway works in Ontario, Alberta and British Columbia as of October 2026, what is on the exam, and how it fits with the CWB qualifications that most structural jobs ask for. It is written for apprentices, career changers and employers posting jobs on HubWeld.

What is a Red Seal welder?

A Red Seal welder holds a provincial or territorial journeyperson certificate in the welder trade with a Red Seal endorsement. The Red Seal Program describes the endorsement as the primary tool for labour mobility across Canada. Certification itself stays provincial: Skilled Trades Ontario, Alberta Apprenticeship and Industry Training and SkilledTradesBC each run their own apprenticeships and issue their own certificates.

In occupational statistics, Red Seal welders fall under NOC 2021 code 72106, "Welders and related machine operators", which also includes apprentices and machine welding operators. That is the code to look up when you check wages or job outlooks on Job Bank. Our article on welder salaries in Canada by province uses the same data.

How to become a Red Seal welder

The path has the same shape in every province, even though hours and level names differ.

  1. Find an employer sponsor. An apprenticeship is a work contract. You need an employer willing to register you, usually after you have some pre-apprenticeship or college training.
  2. Register as an apprentice with the provincial authority.
  3. Log your on-the-job hours under a journeyperson's supervision, and get them signed off.
  4. Complete each level of in-school (technical) training and pass the level exams.
  5. Write the Red Seal interprovincial exam once your hours and levels are complete.
  6. Receive your certificate of qualification with the Red Seal endorsement.

Experienced welders who never apprenticed can usually "challenge" the exam by proving enough documented hours in the trade. The hour requirement for challengers is higher than for apprentices; British Columbia, for example, asks for 6,930 hours.

Ontario: welder 456A

Ontario's welder trade is non-compulsory, which means you can legally work as a welder without the certificate. In practice, many employers, unions and public-sector contracts still prefer or require it. Skilled Trades Ontario's 2025 trade report counted 51 certificates issued that year, a small number for a province with roughly 28,800 people in the occupation. It suggests most Ontario welders work on CWB tickets and experience rather than a trade certificate, and it also means a Red Seal still sets you apart in a hiring pile.

Alberta

Alberta's welder apprenticeship is three periods of one year, each with at least 1,560 hours of work and 8 weeks of technical training. Alberta also offers related programs, such as wire process operator, for welders who work mainly with semi-automatic processes. Check the province's trade rules before you start, since they affect who can do which work.

British Columbia

BC replaced its old Foundation, Level C, B and A system with a harmonized three-level program that matches the national Red Seal standard. The three technical levels are 240, 240 and 300 hours, and you need 70% on each level's exam. The Red Seal exam is written at the end of Level 3. Some colleges still use the old level names in course listings, so confirm which program an intake actually leads to.

What is on the Red Seal welder exam?

The Red Seal welder exam is 125 multiple-choice questions, and you need 70% to pass. Questions are weighted by how much of the job each area represents, according to the Red Seal Occupational Standard for welders.

BLayout and fabrication2822%

Block D covers SMAW, GMAW, FCAW, GTAW and related processes: equipment setup, filler selection, joint design, distortion control and defects. Expect questions that ask why a weld failed as well as what the defect is called. Reading blueprints and welding symbols shows up across blocks A and B, and so does knowing how a procedure is documented; our guide on how to read a WPS is useful revision.

Tips for passing

  • Study by block weight. Welding processes are almost half the marks.
  • Use the exam counselling sheet for your province, which lists the sub-tasks behind each block.
  • Practise metal and electrode math (heat input, consumable weight, unit conversions) until it is automatic.
  • Review the defects and acceptance criteria you see at work. Our article on weld inspection and NDT methods covers the vocabulary.

CWB ticket vs Red Seal: what is the difference?

A CWB ticket proves you can make a specific weld today; a Red Seal proves you have learned the trade. A CWB qualification is a practical test under CSA W47.1 (steel), W47.2 (aluminum) or W186 (rebar), tied to a process and a classification, and witnessed by a CWB representative. It usually lasts two years and depends on continuous employment with a CWB-certified company. A Red Seal is earned once through an apprenticeship and does not expire.

Is a Red Seal worth it for welders?

For most welders who plan to stay in the trade, yes. Job Bank's national outlook for welders is a "moderate risk of shortage" through 2033, and about a quarter of the workforce was 50 or older in 2023. A Red Seal moves you from apprentice to journeyperson pay in most collective agreements, and it lets you work in another province without re-certifying. That matters if you want to follow LNG, nuclear or shipbuilding work; we map those projects in welding jobs in Canada for 2026.

It is less essential if you plan to stay with one fabrication shop on CWB tickets, or if you run a small mobile welding business where customers care more about your reviews than your certificate. Even then, it is one of the few credentials a customer can check.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours do you need to become a Red Seal welder in Ontario?

Ontario's welder 456A apprenticeship is about 6,000 hours in total: roughly 5,280 hours on the job and 720 hours of in-school training across three levels. After completing the hours and levels, you write the Red Seal interprovincial exam.

How many questions are on the Red Seal welder exam?

The exam has 125 multiple-choice questions in four blocks: common occupational skills (20), layout and fabrication (28), cutting and gouging (23) and welding processes (54). You need 70% to pass.

Is a Red Seal the same as a CWB ticket?

No. A CWB ticket is a practical qualification for a specific process and weld type, usually valid for two years and tied to employment with a CWB-certified company. A Red Seal is a trade certificate earned through an apprenticeship and an exam, and it does not expire.

Can I challenge the Red Seal welder exam without an apprenticeship?

Most provinces let experienced welders challenge the exam if they can document enough hours in the trade. The requirement is higher than for apprentices. In British Columbia, for example, challengers need 6,930 hours. Check with your provincial apprenticeship authority.

Do I need a Red Seal to work as a welder in Ontario?

No. Welder 456A is a non-compulsory trade in Ontario, so you can work without the certificate. Many employers, unions and public contracts still prefer it, and structural work will usually also require a CWB ticket.

Does a Red Seal let me work in other provinces?

Yes. The Red Seal endorsement is how other provinces and territories recognise your certificate, so you can work as a journeyperson welder across Canada without re-testing for the trade certificate. Job-specific CWB tickets are separate.
