Is there a welder shortage in Canada?

There is a moderate national shortage on paper, but not everywhere. Job Bank's national outlook for welders (NOC 72106) rates 2024–2033 as a "moderate risk of shortage". The Canadian Occupational Projection System, summarised by the CWB's welding education snapshot, projects about 25,600 job openings against about 25,000 job seekers over that period.

The gap is small. Most of the openings come from retirements rather than growth: employment in the occupation has fallen about 1.3% a year over the past decade, 26% of welders were 50 or older in 2023, and the median retirement age is 64. Women make up only 3.9% of the workforce.

Census 2021 counted roughly 96,000 people in the welder occupation group, and Job Bank put 2023 employment at 90,900. That is a mid-sized trade, so a single large project can change the local market fast.

Welding jobs in Ontario

Ontario has the most welders of any province, about 28,800 according to Job Bank, and the weakest outlook. The 2025–2027 Ontario outlook is "very limited": employment is declining, a few positions will open through retirement, and several welders are already unemployed. Manufacturing and auto supply chains carry much of the province's welding employment, and they are the sectors most exposed to U.S. tariffs.

The bright spots are specialised. Nuclear work at Darlington and Bruce, energy infrastructure and public construction need code-qualified welders, and those employers screen hard for CWB tickets and Red Seal status. Which industries are hiring welders in Canada?

Big projects create welding demand in waves: fabrication first, then field installation, then maintenance. These are the main drivers as of October 2026.

LNG Canada Phase 2 (British Columbia)

Shell and its partners took the final investment decision on LNG Canada Phase 2 on September 29, 2026. The expansion adds two liquefaction trains, taking capacity from 14 to 28 million tonnes a year, with operations expected in the early 2030s. The federal government described it as the second-largest private investment in Canadian history. Expect demand for pipe, pressure and structural welders to build over the next several years, both on site in Kitimat and in module and spool fabrication shops.

Nuclear construction and refurbishment (Ontario)

The first BWRX-300 small modular reactor at Darlington is under construction. The Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission issued the construction licence in April 2025 and cleared the reactor building foundation hold point on March 30, 2026. Up to four units are planned. At Bruce Power, Unit 3 returned to service in June 2026 and Unit 4 is in refurbishment, with major component replacements planned through 2033.

One project has wrapped up: OPG finished the Darlington refurbishment when Unit 4 returned to service in March 2026. Experienced trades from that job are now looking for the next one.

Shipbuilding

The National Shipbuilding Strategy keeps work flowing to Irving in Halifax, Seaspan in Vancouver and Davie in Quebec. Shipyards employ large numbers of structural welders, and long-running build programs give them steadier hiring than one-off construction projects.

Battery and manufacturing plants

Electric-vehicle battery plants created construction welding work in Ontario, but timelines have slipped. Volkswagen's PowerCo plant in St. Thomas has delayed production to 2029, though construction continues. Treat these as construction-phase jobs rather than long-term production welding.

How U.S. tariffs affect welding jobs in Canada

Tariffs hit fabricators from both sides: export orders get more expensive for U.S. buyers, and imported steel gets more expensive for Canadian shops. As of October 2026:

  • The United States has applied a 50% tariff to about $27.6 billion of Canadian goods since August 22, 2026.
  • Canada matched it from September 8, 2026, raising counter-tariffs on U.S. steel and aluminum from 25% to 50%.
  • Since December 26, 2025, Canada has limited steel imports with quotas and charges a 50% surtax above quota, plus a 25% surtax on steel derivative products.
  • Statistics Canada's Industrial Product Price Index was up 12.4% year over year in June 2026.

For welders, the practical effect is that shops exporting to the U.S. are cutting hours or pausing hiring, while shops working on Canadian infrastructure, energy and defence are busier. Buy-Canadian procurement could shift more fabrication to domestic shops over time, but there is no reliable count of jobs that has produced yet. How to find welding jobs in Canada in 2026

  • Follow the projects, not the headlines. Contractors on LNG, nuclear and shipbuilding work post openings months before peak hiring. Watch their career pages and union dispatch boards.
  • Hold the tickets those projects ask for. A current CWB ticket in the right process, and pressure tickets for pipe work, matter more than any résumé wording.
  • Consider rotation or relocation. If Ontario is slow, Atlantic Canada and the West are hiring for specific trades.
  • Fill gaps with contract work. Mobile repairs, small fabrication and structural jobs from local customers keep income steady between big projects. Our article on growing a mobile welding business has practical ideas.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a shortage of welders in Canada in 2026?

Job Bank rates the national outlook for 2024–2033 as a moderate risk of shortage, with about 25,600 openings and 25,000 job seekers. The shortage is uneven: PEI and Nova Scotia are rated good, while Ontario is rated very limited.

Which province has the most welding jobs?

Ontario has the most welders, about 28,800, but its outlook is very limited. Prince Edward Island and Nova Scotia have the best outlook ratings. Alberta and British Columbia are rated limited but pay among the highest median wages.

Why is the welding job outlook in Ontario so weak?

Job Bank cites declining employment, few retirement openings and a number of unemployed welders already looking. Ontario's manufacturing sector is also exposed to U.S. tariffs, and the Darlington nuclear refurbishment finished in March 2026.

What projects are hiring welders in Canada?

LNG Canada Phase 2 in British Columbia, which reached a final investment decision on September 29, 2026; the Darlington small modular reactor and Bruce Power refurbishments in Ontario; and federal shipbuilding programs at Irving, Seaspan and Davie.

How are U.S. tariffs affecting welders in Canada?

A 50% U.S. tariff on many Canadian goods since August 22, 2026 has hurt export-focused fabricators, while Canadian counter-tariffs and steel import quotas raise steel costs for shops. Domestic infrastructure and energy work has been steadier.

How many welders are there in Canada?

Job Bank reports about 90,900 people employed as welders and related machine operators in 2023. Census 2021 counted roughly 96,000 in the occupation group, including apprentices.
