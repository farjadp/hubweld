Most welders who want side work or their own contracts get it the same way: word of mouth, a Facebook group, or a general contractor who calls back when they're short. That works until it doesn't. HubWeld gives you a second channel, a job board where customers across Canada post welding work and welders bid on it. This guide covers how to set up a profile that gets picked, how to bid, and how to build the reviews that win the next job.

Who posts welding contracts on HubWeld?

Welding jobs on HubWeld are posted by customers: facility and maintenance managers, small contractors, farmers, property owners and fabricators who need extra hands. Every job falls into one of five categories, which you can filter on the open welding jobs board:

  • Mobile welding for on-site repairs, equipment, gates and trailers
  • Fabrication for brackets, frames, railings and custom parts
  • Repair for cracked castings, broken frames and worn parts
  • Structural for beams, columns and connections
  • Other for anything that doesn't fit, from art to prototypes

Only jobs with the Open status appear on the board. Once a customer accepts a bid, the job comes off the list.

Step 1: Register as a welder

Go to sign up as a welder on HubWeld. The form asks for your name, email, a password (at least six characters) and your city. You're signed in right away and sent to your dashboard, where the profile editor is.

Step 2: Build a profile that wins welding contracts

Your profile is what customers look at when your bid lands. Many welders lose work here, not on price. A customer comparing three bids of similar value will pick the one with photos, a clear skill list and a ticket they recognize.

The profile editor has these fields, and here is how to use each one:

FieldWhat to put in it
PhotoA clear photo of you, helmet up, or of your truck setup. Not a logo pulled off the internet.
BioThree or four sentences: years in the trade, the kind of work you do best, and whether you work mobile, from a shop or both.
SkillsComma-separated, specific: "TIG stainless, MIG, flux-core structural, aluminum boat repair, pipe 6G". Customers search on these words.
CertificationsExactly as on your card: "CWB W47.1 SMAW all-position T", "Red Seal Welder (Ontario 456A)". Add the expiry if it helps.

Country and countries you serveYour country of residence and anywhere else you can take work.
Local service areaThe towns you'll actually drive to: "Hamilton, Burlington, Niagara".
Hourly rate (CAD)Your real rate. Customers compare it against your bid.
Years of experienceHonest number. It shows in your sidebar.
ResumeOne page, tickets and recent jobs. Customers can open it from your profile.

Customers search the welder directory by name, skill or certification and filter by city or service area. If your skills and service area use the words people search for, you show up. "Stainless" will match a search for stainless; "SS" probably won't.

Your certifications are a claim until the customer sees the card

The certifications field is free text, and HubWeld does not check it. Serious customers will ask for your CWB wallet card or Red Seal certificate. Have a photo of both on your phone so you can answer in minutes. Step 3: Get the Verified badge

The Verified badge is set by the HubWeld team after they review a completed profile. Fill in every field, upload a resume and some gallery photos, then contact the team through your dashboard to request a review. Verified welders get a badge on their profile and in the directory, and they're listed ahead of unverified welders.

Unverified welders can still bid on every open job. The badge just makes the customer's choice easier.

Step 4: Find jobs worth bidding on

Filter the jobs board by your city and the categories you're good at. Read the whole description before you bid. Look for:

  • Material and thickness, and whether it suits your machine and process
  • Whether a code applies or a CWB-certified company is required
  • Site access: power, hot-work permits, safety orientation, heights
  • Who supplies material
  • The timeline, and whether you can meet it
  • The budget, if the customer gave one

If the description leaves out something you need to price it, ask in a message before you bid (see Step 6).

Step 5: How to price and write a welding bid

Each welder gets one bid per job on HubWeld. The bid has two parts: an amount in whole Canadian dollars and a message of up to 2,000 characters. You can't revise and resubmit, so price it properly the first time.

Pricing the bid

Build your number from the bottom up:

  1. Labour. Hours on site or in the shop, times your rate.
  2. Travel. Drive time and kilometres, or a flat call-out fee.
  3. Material and consumables. Steel, filler, gas, grinding discs. Say whether these are included.
  4. Risk. Unknowns like rusted-through base metal, tight access, or a part that may need to come off.

To check your rate against the market, look at our breakdown of welder salary in Canada by province, which uses Job Bank's wage data for NOC 72106, and at mobile welding rates in 2026 for what contractors charge. Remember that a contract rate has to cover your truck, machine, insurance and the jobs you quote but don't get. Matching an employee's hourly wage is not enough.

Writing the bid message

The message is your sales pitch. Keep it short and specific:

Price includes 2 hours on site, travel from Burlington, ER308L filler and argon. I'll TIG the rail with a purge and blend it flush. CWB W47.1 GTAW, card on request. I can do Tuesday or Thursday after 2 p.m. Material is your existing rail, no new tube needed unless the crack runs into the post.

That message answers the five things a customer compares: what the price includes, the process, the ticket, availability and the risk.

Step 6: Use messages to win the job

Every job has a private message thread between the customer and each bidder. You see only your own conversation, never another welder's. Use it to:

  • Ask for photos or dimensions the description left out
  • Send a photo of your CWB card when asked
  • Confirm the payment terms: deposit, final amount, e-transfer or invoice

HubWeld does not process payments for jobs. Agreeing on terms in the thread gives you a written record if there's a disagreement later.

Step 7: After you win the contract

When the customer accepts your bid, it's marked accepted and the job moves to Assigned. Every other bid is rejected at the same time. When you start the work, mark the job In progress. The customer marks it Completed when the work is done.

The HubWeld mobile app, which is coming soon, sends push notifications for new messages, accepted bids and job status changes. On the website, check your dashboard and your jobs regularly so a customer's question doesn't sit unanswered.

Step 8: Build reviews that win the next job

After a job is marked Completed, the customer can rate you from 1 to 5 stars and leave a comment. It shows on your profile with your average rating, and your last ten reviews are visible to everyone. Within each page of the directory, welders are sorted by average rating.

The first three or four reviews do more for you than anything else on your profile. Some ways to earn them:

  • Show up when you said you would, or message ahead if you can't.
  • Clean up: grinding dust, rod stubs and wire offcuts.
  • Send a photo of the finished weld in the job thread.
  • Ask the customer, politely, to leave a review once they mark the job complete.

Frequently asked questions

Is HubWeld free for welders?

Yes. Registering as a welder, building a profile and bidding on open jobs on HubWeld costs nothing. You agree payment terms directly with the customer in the job's message thread, because HubWeld does not process payments for jobs.

Can I change my bid after I submit it?

No. Each welder can submit one bid per job, with an amount in Canadian dollars and a message. Price carefully, and if the job description is missing information you need, ask the customer in a message before you bid.

How do I get the Verified badge?

Complete every field of your welder profile, including a resume and gallery photos, then contact the HubWeld team through your dashboard to ask for a review. An admin approves the profile and the badge appears. Verified welders are listed first in the directory.

Do I need a CWB ticket to bid on HubWeld jobs?

No, but many customers ask for one, especially for structural or code work. List your tickets exactly as written on your card in the certifications field, and keep a photo of the card on your phone to send when a customer asks.

How do customers find my profile?

Customers see your profile when you bid on their job, and they can find you in the welder directory by searching a name, skill or certification and filtering by city or service area. Use plain, specific skill words like stainless, aluminum and pipe so your profile matches their searches.

Can welders review customers?

No. On HubWeld, only the customer who posted the job can leave a review, and only after the job is marked Completed. Use the message thread to keep a clear record of what was agreed in case something goes wrong.
