Finding someone to weld a cracked trailer hitch or a run of handrail is not hard. Finding the right person, at a fair price, who shows up with the right ticket, is where most people lose a week. This guide walks through how to hire a welder on HubWeld from the first post to the final review, and what to check at each step so the job goes the way you planned.

Before you post: decide what kind of welder you need

The kind of welder you need depends on where the work happens and whether a code applies. A mobile welder comes to you with a truck-mounted machine. A fabrication shop builds parts on its own floor and delivers them. Structural work on a building usually needs a company certified by the Canadian Welding Bureau (CWB) to CSA W47.1, with welders qualified under that company.

HubWeld's job categories follow the same split, so picking the right one helps the right people find your post:

If the work involves anything load-bearing on a building, a pressure system or a bridge, read our guide to CWB certification in Canada before you post. It explains what a CWB ticket covers and what the FW, S and T classifications mean, so you know what to ask for.

How to post a welding job on HubWeld, step by step

Posting a job on HubWeld takes about five minutes and costs nothing. You need a Customer account, and the post goes live on the public job board as soon as you submit it.

Step 1: Create a Customer account

Go to create a HubWeld account and choose "I need welding work". You will be asked for your name, email, a password and, optionally, your city. You are signed in straight away and taken to your dashboard.

Step 2: Open the job form

Go to post a welding job. The form has five fields.

  • Title. One line that names the work and the material, for example "Stainless rail repair at food plant" or "Replace 6 m of 3 in. sch 40 steel pipe support".
  • Description. The full scope. More on what to include below.
  • City. Where the work is, or where the part needs to be delivered. Welders filter the job board by city, so use the city name people would search for, such as Mississauga rather than "west GTA".
  • Category. Mobile welding, fabrication, repair, structural or other.
  • Budget (CAD). Optional, in whole dollars. A realistic number attracts serious bids. Leaving it blank is fine if you have no idea what the job costs.

Step 3: Submit and wait for bids

Your job appears on the welding jobs board with the status Open. Welders can see it, filter by city and category, and send a bid. Each welder can bid only once on your job, so the price you see is their real number, not an opening offer.

How to write a welding job description that gets accurate bids

A good job description gives a welder enough detail to price the job without a site visit. Vague posts get vague bids, or high ones, because the welder has to cover what they cannot see.

Cover these points:

  1. Material and thickness. Mild steel, stainless (304 or 316 if you know), aluminum, cast iron. Give thickness in inches or millimetres: 1/4 in. plate, 11 gauge sheet, 3/16 in. wall tube.
  2. What is broken or needs building. Length of the crack, number of brackets, dimensions of the frame.
  3. Process, if it matters. Food-plant stainless usually means TIG. Outdoor structural repair is often stick or flux-core. If you are not sure, say so. Our comparison of MIG vs TIG vs stick welding covers which process suits which job.
  4. Code or certification needs. Say whether the work falls under CSA W59 or another standard, whether you need a CWB-certified company, and which ticket classification you expect. If an engineer or inspector will sign off, say that too.
  5. Site access. Indoors or outdoors, power available or not, hot-work permit, fire watch, safety orientation, working at heights, parking for a welding truck.
  6. Who supplies material. You or the welder.
  7. Timeline. When the work must be done and any shutdown windows.

Here is an example of a description that will get comparable bids:

Cracked weld on a 304 stainless handrail, 2 in. OD x 0.065 in. wall tube, about 40 cm of joint across three posts. Food-processing plant in Guelph, indoors, 120 V outlet within 10 m. Hot-work permit and a 30-minute safety orientation on arrival. TIG with purge preferred, finish ground and blended. Weekday after 2 p.m., any day next week. We do not supply filler.

How much should you budget to hire a welder?

What you pay to hire a welder in Canada depends on the work type, travel and urgency. A mobile welder usually charges a call-out or travel fee plus an hourly rate. A shop may quote a fixed price for fabrication.

The lowest bid is not automatically the best one. Compare each bid against the same five points, and look at the welder's public profile before you decide.

On each welder's profile you will see their bio, skills, certifications, languages, service area, years of experience, hourly rate, a photo gallery of past work and, if they uploaded one, a resume. A "Verified" badge means the HubWeld team has reviewed and approved that profile. It does not mean HubWeld has checked their welding tickets.

Check certifications yourself

The certifications field on a HubWeld profile is free text the welder fills in. Treat it as a claim to verify, not proof. For CWB work, ask the welder for their CWB welder ID wallet card and look at the process, the classification and the expiry date. Most CWB qualifications last two years as long as the welder stays employed by a certified company (CWB welder qualification). If the card has expired or covers a different process than your job needs, ask before you accept.

A welder who also holds a provincial trade certificate has finished an apprenticeship; our guide to the Red Seal welder credential explains what that involves and how it differs from a CWB ticket. For a deeper checklist on vetting welders, see hiring certified welders in 2026.

Ask questions before you accept

Every HubWeld job has a message thread. Before you award the job, you pick which bidder to write to, and only that welder sees the conversation. Bidders never see each other's messages.

Questions worth asking:

  • Does your price include material, filler, gas and travel?
  • What process and filler will you use?
  • Can you send a photo of your CWB card, or of a similar job you've done?
  • Do you carry liability insurance and WSIB or provincial workers' compensation coverage?
  • How long will the work take on site, and do you need anything from us?

Accept a bid and track the job

When you click Accept on a bid, three things happen at once: that bid is marked accepted, every other bid on the job is marked rejected, and your job moves from Open to Assigned. It also comes off the public job board.

From there the job moves through these statuses:

StatusMeaningWho can move it
OpenVisible on the job board, taking bidsSet when you post

In progressWork has startedThe assigned welder, you, or an admin

CancelledThe job is offYou or an admin, any time before Completed

Payment is arranged between you and the welder. HubWeld does not hold or process money for jobs, so agree on a deposit, the final amount and how you'll pay (e-transfer, cheque or invoice) in the message thread before work starts. Having it in writing there protects both sides.

Leave a review when the work is done

Once you mark the job Completed, you can rate the welder from 1 to 5 stars and add a comment. You get one review per job, and it shows on the welder's public profile. Be specific: "Arrived on time, purged the stainless properly, cleaned up" helps the next customer more than "Great job".

Frequently asked questions

Does it cost anything to post a welding job on HubWeld?

No. Creating a Customer account and posting a job on HubWeld is free. You pay the welder you hire directly, on terms you agree in the job's message thread. HubWeld does not process payments for jobs, so settle the deposit, final amount and payment method with the welder before work starts.

How do I know if a welder is CWB certified?

Ask for their CWB welder ID wallet card and check the process, classification (FW, S or T) and expiry date. The certifications listed on a HubWeld profile are entered by the welder and are not checked by HubWeld. Most CWB qualifications are valid for two years while the welder stays with a CWB-certified company.

What does the Verified badge on a welder's profile mean?

It means the HubWeld team has reviewed and approved that welder's profile. Verified welders appear first in the directory. The badge is not a certification check, so you should still verify tickets, insurance and workers' compensation coverage yourself for any job that needs them.

Can I talk to a welder before I accept their bid?

Yes. Each job has a message thread. Before you award the job, you choose which bidder to message, and only that welder sees the conversation. Use it to confirm what the price includes, the process and filler, the schedule and how payment will work.

What happens to the other bids when I accept one?

When you accept a bid, it becomes the accepted bid, every other bid on the job is marked rejected, and the job moves from Open to Assigned. The job then disappears from the public job board, so no new bids come in.

Can I cancel a job after I post it?

Yes. You can cancel a job at any point before it is marked Completed. Messaging closes on cancelled jobs, so tell the assigned welder first if you have already accepted a bid.
