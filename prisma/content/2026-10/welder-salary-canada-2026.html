The median welder salary in Canada is $30.00 an hour, according to Job Bank's wage data for NOC 72106 (updated November 2025). That works out to roughly $60,000 a year at 2,000 hours. The low end is about $22 an hour and the high end about $47. Alberta, British Columbia, Newfoundland and Labrador and the territories pay the most; Ontario and Quebec sit near $28.

Two things stand out. The western and northern provinces pay more at the median, largely because of resource, pipeline and heavy industrial work. And the spread between low and high is wide everywhere: in New Brunswick a top-end welder earns more than double an entry-level one.

How much do welders make in Ontario?

An Ontario welder earns a median of $28.00 an hour, with a range of $21.00 to $41.28, according to Job Bank. Skilled Trades Ontario's 2025 trade report for welder (456A) gives a slightly higher band of $28 to $39 an hour, or about $54,000 to $76,000 a year. The STO figure describes the certified trade, so it skews toward journeypersons rather than production welders.

Ontario is the largest welding labour market in the country, but it is also the softest right now. Job Bank rates the province's three-year outlook for welders as "very limited", which means more qualified people than openings in many regions. That matters for pay: in a crowded market, the welders who earn above the median are the ones with something specific to sell, such as pipe tickets, stainless and sanitary work, or a reputation for showing up. Welder pay in Alberta and British Columbia

Alberta has the highest median of any province at $38.00 an hour, and its high end reaches $52.18. British Columbia follows at $35.00, with a high of $51.87. Both numbers are pulled up by oil and gas, pipeline, mining and large industrial construction, where pressure-pipe welders on rotation can earn well above the published high once overtime and living-out allowances are counted.

The federal outlook for both provinces is "limited", so the high wages are not a sign of easy hiring. Employers in those sectors pay for specific qualifications and are picky about them. A general shop welder moving to Calgary without a pressure ticket may land close to the provincial median, not the top of it.

What raises a welder's salary?

A welder's hourly rate climbs fastest with qualifications that are expensive to get and hard to fake. Here is what tends to move pay in Canada, roughly in order of impact:

  1. Pressure and pipe qualifications. Pipe welding to provincial pressure codes is the classic route to the high end of the wage table. It takes practice, test fees and nerve, which is why it pays.
  2. CWB tickets in more processes and positions. A CWB qualification under CSA W47.1 is tied to a process and a classification (fillet only, or full-penetration from one side without backing). Each added process or classification widens the jobs you can take. See our step-by-step CWB certification guide.
  3. Red Seal journeyperson status. The Red Seal endorsement is how employers in other provinces recognise your trade certificate, and many collective agreements pay journeypersons more than apprentices. Our Red Seal welder guide covers hours and the exam.
  4. Sector. Nuclear, LNG, heavy industrial and shipbuilding projects usually pay more than general fabrication because of their inspection requirements and remote locations.
  5. Remote, camp and rotation work. Northern and remote sites pay premiums and often a living-out allowance. The Northwest Territories median of $48.29 reflects that.
  6. Overtime and shift premiums. On shutdowns and turnarounds, 12-hour days are common. Your annual income can far exceed the hourly median × 2,000.
  7. Union membership. Building trades unions negotiate scales by local and by sector. Rates are set in collective agreements, so ask the local directly. For comparison, Job Bank lists the Ontario boilermaker median at $48.21/hr, a related trade that does a lot of pressure welding.

Apprentice vs journeyperson welder pay

Apprentices in Canada are paid a percentage of the journeyperson rate that rises with each level they complete. The exact percentages depend on the province and, for union apprentices, the collective agreement. As a rough guide, the "low" column in the table above, $20 to $25 an hour in most provinces, is where new and apprentice welders tend to start.

Finishing the apprenticeship and passing the Red Seal exam is the single biggest jump most welders see in their first five years. In Ontario, the 456A trade takes about 6,000 hours of combined work and school time.

How to raise your rate as a welder

Waiting for an annual raise is the slowest way to earn more. Welders who move up quickly usually do some of the following.

Add a ticket that local employers keep asking for

Read 20 job ads in your region and count the qualifications they mention. If half of them want a CWB all-position ticket in FCAW and you only hold flat and horizontal, that test is worth more to you than a new helmet. Test fees run about $170 to $185 per position at many Canadian test centres.

Document your work

A short portfolio of photos (before, root pass, cap, finished part) and a list of the codes you have worked to does more in a hiring call than a résumé line. On HubWeld you can attach a résumé, a project gallery and your certifications to your welder directory profile.

Take contract and side work

Many welders top up a salaried job with mobile repairs, railings, trailer hitches and farm equipment. Rates for that work are set by the job, not by an employer's pay band. Our breakdown of mobile welding rates shows how others price it, and finding welding contracts on HubWeld explains how bidding works on the platform.

Move, or rotate, to where the work is

A $10/hr difference between Ontario and Alberta medians is about $20,000 a year before overtime. Rotation work lets some welders earn western rates without moving their family. Weigh it against cost of living and time away.

What the 2026 market means for welder pay

Job Bank's national outlook for 2024–2033 is a "moderate risk of shortage", and 26% of welders were 50 or older in 2023. Retirements will open positions over the next decade. In the short term, though, the picture is uneven. Ontario's manufacturing base is under pressure from U.S. tariffs, while LNG Canada Phase 2 and nuclear work are creating demand for specific, highly qualified welders.

Translated into pay: general production welding rates may stay flat in 2026, while pipe, pressure and code-qualified welders have more leverage than the wage tables suggest. Employers looking to hire can read our guide to hiring certified welders for the employer side of the same picture.

What is the average welder salary in Canada?

Which province pays welders the most?

Among the provinces, Alberta has the highest median at $38.00 an hour, followed closely by Newfoundland and Labrador at $38.41 and British Columbia at $35.00. The Northwest Territories has the highest median overall at $48.29, reflecting remote and northern work.

How much does a welder make in Ontario per year?

Do Red Seal welders earn more?

Usually, yes. Completing the apprenticeship and earning the Red Seal moves you from apprentice to journeyperson pay, which is set higher in most collective agreements and employer pay bands. The endorsement also lets other provinces recognise your certificate, which opens higher-paying markets.

Is welding a good career in Canada in 2026?

Job Bank rates the national 10-year outlook as a moderate risk of shortage, and about a quarter of welders are 50 or older. Short-term demand is uneven: Ontario's outlook is very limited, while pipe, pressure and code-qualified welders are in demand on LNG and nuclear projects.
