Choosing the right category

The shop's categories are the same ones buyers browse: welding machines (MIG, TIG, stick, multi-process, plasma cutters, engine drives, wire feeders), consumables, gas and regulators, safety gear, tools and accessories, and abrasives. A buyer looking for TIG torch parts or MIG welders lands on that category page, so a product in the wrong one is a product they won't see.

How to write welding product listings that sell

Buyers of welding equipment are specific. A maintenance planner wants to know whether a contact tip fits their gun. A contractor wants to know whether a machine runs on the 240 V outlet in their truck. A good listing answers the question before the buyer has to call.

Put the specs in the description

For machines, include the input voltage and phase, output range, rated duty cycle (for example, 40% at 200 A), processes supported, weight and what's in the box. For consumables, give the size, the AWS or CSA classification, package quantity and what it fits. For PPE, list the standard it meets, such as CSA Z94.3 for welding filters. Our guide on sizing a welder by duty cycle explains which numbers buyers compare.

Note the certification mark on electrical equipment

Electrical equipment sold in Canada must carry a mark from a certification body accredited by the Standards Council of Canada. In Ontario, the Ontario regulation on electrical product safety says no one may sell an electrical product unless it has been approved that way. State the certification mark (for example, CSA) in the description. Buyers who know the rule look for it, and our welding machine buying guide tells them to.

Use your real SKU

Maintenance buyers often search by part number. Put the manufacturer's part number in the SKU field and repeat it in the description so it's easy to find.

Keep stock accurate

Stock on HubWeld is reserved the moment a buyer checks out. If your listing says 12 and you have 2, you'll be cancelling lines and disappointing buyers. Update stock when you sell through other channels, and set a listing to Draft or Archived when you can't ship it.

Use clear photos you're allowed to use

One clear photo of the actual product on a plain background beats a busy lifestyle shot. Only use images you have the right to use: your own photos, or manufacturer images your agreement with the brand allows.

Step 3: Receive and fulfil orders

When a buyer places an order that includes your products, three things happen:

  1. The order is recorded with the status Pending and your stock is reserved.
  2. You get an email: "HubWeld: new order" with the order reference.
  3. The buyer and the HubWeld operations team get their own emails. The team follows up with the buyer to arrange payment, either by contact or by Net 30 invoice for approved business accounts.

HubWeld's checkout doesn't charge a card online. The buyer chooses to be contacted about payment or to request a Net 30 invoice, so expect the operations team to confirm payment arrangements before you ship.

Your orders appear in the supplier dashboard under Orders. An order can include items from several suppliers, but you see and manage only your own lines. Set each line to:

Step 4: Read your supplier dashboard

The supplier dashboard gives you four numbers at a glance:

  • Total products: every listing you've created, in any status
  • Active listings: products buyers can see and order right now
  • Items sold: units on orders that have shipped or been completed
  • Gross revenue: the value of those sold items in CAD

It also lists recent order lines, so you can jump straight to anything waiting to ship.

Step 5: Get the Verified Supplier badge

New supplier accounts show "Pending verification" on the dashboard. To get verified, complete your profile and contact the HubWeld team to request a review. Once an admin approves your account, a "Verified Supplier" badge appears on your product pages and a Verified badge on your dashboard.

You can list and sell before you're verified. The badge tells buyers that the HubWeld team has reviewed your business, which matters when they're ordering a machine from a supplier they haven't bought from before.

Frequently asked questions

How do I become a supplier on HubWeld?

Go to the HubWeld registration page, choose "I sell welding supplies", and enter a contact name, your business name, email and password. You're signed in right away and can add products from the supplier dashboard. To get the Verified Supplier badge, complete your profile and ask the HubWeld team for a review.

What currency do I list prices in?

All HubWeld prices are entered and settled in Canadian dollars. Buyers can view indicative conversions to US dollars or euros, but every order total and invoice is in CAD.

How does HubWeld handle payment for my orders?

The HubWeld website does not charge buyers' cards. At checkout a buyer chooses either to be contacted to arrange payment or to request a Net 30 invoice if they have an approved business account. The order is recorded as Pending, your stock is reserved, and the operations team follows up with the buyer.

What happens to my stock when an order is cancelled?

When you cancel one of your order lines, or a buyer cancels a Pending order, the reserved quantity is added back to your stock automatically.

Can I sell before I'm verified?

Yes. Verification isn't required to list or sell products on HubWeld. Verified suppliers get a "Verified Supplier" badge on their product pages, which helps buyers who don't know your business yet.

Do welding machines I sell need a certification mark?

Yes. Electrical equipment sold in Canada needs a mark from a certification body accredited by the Standards Council of Canada, such as CSA. Ontario's electrical product safety regulation prohibits selling unapproved electrical products. Put the mark in your product description.
